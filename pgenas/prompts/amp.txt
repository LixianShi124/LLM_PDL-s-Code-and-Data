Module 1: Role Setup & Resource Constraints

You are an expert neural architecture designer specializing in lightweight neural networks for industrial power quality disturbance identification.

Task:
Design a compact PyTorch neural network for amplitude-related disturbance identification.

Target classes:
0: Non-amplitude disturbance
1: Voltage swell
2: Voltage sag
3: Voltage interruption

Hard constraints:
- Total trainable parameters must not exceed 50,000.
- Network FLOPs for one input sample must not exceed 50 million.
- Both constraints will be checked by an external evaluator.

Optimization objectives:
- Improve validation classification accuracy.
- Reduce network FLOPs while preserving classification performance.
- The two objectives will be evaluated using Pareto-based selection.

Module 2: Physics-Informed Prior

The amplitude branch receives two synchronized voltage representations:

Channel 1: x_fund, the filtered fundamental-frequency waveform.
Channel 2: x_env, the corresponding amplitude envelope.

Each channel contains 1200 temporal samples.

The inputs are constructed using the TFM-FrZHAPE preprocessing procedure.

Physical characteristics:
- Voltage sag produces a temporary reduction in amplitude.
- Voltage swell produces a temporary increase in amplitude.
- Voltage interruption produces a severe amplitude reduction.
- Event onset, duration, amplitude depth, and temporal boundaries are important for classification.

Architecture guidance:
- Extract complementary information from the fundamental waveform and amplitude envelope.
- Preserve temporal variations and local amplitude transitions.
- Use suitable temporal receptive fields to characterize disturbance durations.
- Avoid excessive early downsampling that may suppress short amplitude events.

Module 3: Resource-Aware Operator Guidance

Prefer computationally efficient operators, including:
- Lightweight 1D convolutions.
- Depthwise separable convolutions.
- Compact residual or bottleneck blocks.
- Global or adaptive pooling.
- Lightweight channel attention when beneficial.

Avoid:
- Large fully connected layers applied to flattened temporal features.
- Unnecessarily wide convolutional channels.
- Excessively deep architectures with limited expected benefit.
- Operations likely to violate the parameter or FLOPs budgets.
